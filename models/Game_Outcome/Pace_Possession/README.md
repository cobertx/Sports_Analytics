# NBA Pace & Possession (PACE)

PACE estimates a matchup's shared possessions per team over 48 minutes and forecasts two opposing scoring efficiencies. The historical inputs are the NBA team box scores already used by the app. Averaging both sides' estimated possessions and accounting for observed game duration puts regulation and overtime results on the same pace scale. ESPN games without team minutes use player minute totals when both sides reconcile to their team box scores. Those games become eligible only after both team and player boxes are available. Games with unknown or conflicting duration are excluded from estimation.

A recency-weighted ridge fit estimates team tempo effects. A separate weighted regression of log points per possession estimates offensive strength, opposing defense, and home advantage. The league intercept is unpenalized. Older seasons receive less weight, and neutral games omit the home advantage. Each calibration game is predicted using results available strictly before that game's start. Held-forward pace errors estimate pace bias and variance. Regulation score errors conditional on observed possessions supply the corresponding scoring bias and paired covariance, avoiding double counting pace variation.

One seeded simulation generates the full-game score pair, margin, and total for every path. Paths with tied regulation scores receive successive five-minute overtime periods with time-scaled pace and scoring uncertainty until they produce a winner. The archived integer counts price moneylines, spreads, game totals, and team totals, retaining exact push mass. The app uses the same masses in its probability charts and uses their quantiles for backtest intervals. When verified availability data supports a score adjustment, the app applies it inside a fresh joint simulation.

The historical adapter estimates possessions from shooting, rebounds and turnovers. Overtime uses a time-scaled approximation, with no separate fit for late-game fouling. The base model does not include lineup scenarios, travel, or rest. Research settings can be tested in NBA Strategy & Research and Backtesting.
